## Key Points

**Why workplace boundaries matter**
- Boundaries are the expectations and needs that help people feel safe and comfortable in relationships — work is a relationship too, and often the one where people struggle most to set limits.
- People fear that setting boundaries will make them look like less of a team player.
- Employers already have built-in boundaries (work hours, vacation policy); boundaries need to be a two-way street.
- Setting boundaries leads to feeling happier, more fulfilled, and less exhausted/overwhelmed at work.
- No one can read your mind — coworkers can't infer your preferences from body language, so needs must be explicitly stated.

**Step 1 — Identify the boundaries you need to set**
- Availability: e.g., set hours you are/aren't reachable, leaving promptly at a set time for personal commitments, logging off fully on weekends.
- Communication style: e.g., preferring phone calls over instant messaging/texting, especially with bosses.
- Feedback style: e.g., needing clear deadlines, or preferring written notes to digest feedback.
- Social interactions: e.g., discomfort with gossip, declining after-work drinks but proposing alternatives (like a yoga class).
- Calendar: e.g., asking people to check before adding meetings.

**Step 2 — Think about how and when to state the boundary**
- Set boundaries as early as possible — ideally during the job-seeking/interview process (e.g., asking about pre-planned vacation dates).
- Orientation/onboarding is a good moment if earlier isn't possible (e.g., confirming a hard stop time when told the workday's hours).
- It's acceptable to set boundaries with yourself once you notice something isn't working (e.g., capping meetings per day, batching email responses in set increments instead of reacting immediately).

**Step 3 — Stick to the boundaries you set**
- Consistency is critical — responding outside stated bounds teaches others the boundary isn't real and can be violated.
- Maintaining a boundary may require restating it more than once, and reminding yourself why you set it.
- Boundaries are "contagious" — consistently modeling them can inspire others to set their own, even if they haven't yet found the courage.
